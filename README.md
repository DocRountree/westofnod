# West of Nod

A Godot 4 exploration demo on an infinite procedural archipelago whose terrain is built from
simulated hydrology. The ground is a continuous heightfield; you can walk, swim, fly and dig
into it (it deforms smoothly).

## Running

Open the folder in **Godot 4.3+** and press Play. The islands are simulated and the terrain
is generated by compute shaders, so you need a Vulkan renderer (Forward+ or Mobile). The
Compatibility renderer won't work. The compute shaders are compiled from source at runtime,
so an export needs `*.glsl` in its non-resource export filter.

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD, Space, Ctrl | move, jump/swim up, sprint |
| F | toggle fly mode (Space / Shift to go up / down) |
| F5 | toggle third-person camera |
| Left mouse | dig terrain |
| Esc | release the mouse |

Trees are switched off for now.

## Terrain pipeline

The terrain is built from hydrology. Each island's landscape is the result of a
landscape-evolution simulation, so its drainage basins, ridges and river network emerge
rather than being drawn. Meanders come from a migration model. A single-pass erosion
filter then adds detail below the simulation's grid size.

**1. Archipelago** (`scripts/archipelago.gd`). A very low-frequency density field
separates open ocean from crowded island chains. Each 6 km cell draws 0–4 candidate
islands, more where it's dense, with radii from a heavy-tailed distribution (140 m islets
to 5 km small continents). A candidate is dropped if a bigger one sits too close. So
spacing ranges from tight clusters to wide open sea. Islands are self-contained: each
one is simulated on its own square grid, and its coastline closes well inside that grid.
That locality is what makes the world infinite, and it should make a later move onto a
sphere straightforward: islands just need local tangent frames.

**2. Landscape evolution** (`scripts/island.gd`, `shaders/island_sim.glsl`, GPU, once per
island, then cached). The island mask is a domain-warped radial falloff, sometimes with
satellite lobes. Tectonic uplift is strong in a ridged mountain core and weak towards
the coast. Against that uplift, fluvial incision follows the stream power law
(E = K·A^0.5·S), plus hillslope diffusion and a talus cap. This runs about 2,400
iterations on up to 224² cells. Following Schott et al. 2023, each iteration is two
parallel passes: a semi-implicit erosion step shared among the downslope neighbours by
multiple-flow-direction weights, and one Jacobi step of drainage-area accumulation. Pits
are raised to their spill point.

**3. Lakes and drainage** (CPU). Landslide dams and glacial hollows are dropped into a
few valleys. A priority flood with epsilon (Barnes et al. 2014) then fills every
depression to its spill point, so each lake gets a level and an outlet. Steepest descent
on the filled surface, followed by a topological sweep, gives the flow tree and the
drainage areas.

**4. Rivers.** A cell with enough drainage area is a river. Width follows hydraulic
geometry (w ∝ √A). Chains run from the sea, a lake or a confluence up each main stem.
Main stems are processed first: smoothed off the grid's eight directions, given a gentle
natural sway, and with tributaries joined onto their final course. River mouths open
into estuaries.

**5. Meanders and oxbows** (`scripts/meander.gd`). Gentle, wide reaches meander by
migration, after Howard & Knutson (1984). The channel moves sideways at a rate set by
its local curvature against an upstream-lagged average of it. That makes bends grow,
skew and travel downstream. Bank erodibility varies in patches. The valley walls confine
the belt, whose width is measured from the room the simulated valley gives. When a neck
closes, the river takes the shortcut. Recent cutoffs become oxbow lakes and old ones
become silted meander scars. The whole belt becomes a flat floodplain with bluffs at its
edge.

**6. Fine terrain** (`shaders/terrain_gen.glsl`, per tile, GPU):
- The island heightfield is sampled bicubically, with its analytic gradient, and the sea
  floor is added below the coast from the signed coast distance.
- The erosion filter, modelled on clayjohn's *Eroded Terrain Noise*, adds detail below
  the grid size. Each octave is gaussian-windowed cosines oriented across the slope, so
  troughs line up as gullies running downhill. Each octave is bent by the gullies
  already carved, which makes them branch. Gully depth scales with slope × wavelength.
  The filter fades out on floodplains, lakes, beaches and at sea.
- Floodplains, channels, oxbows and scars are cut in from the river segments. Lake
  water comes from the grid's lake levels, and river water and flow direction come from
  the nearest channel.

**Seamlessness.** A tile receives the cropped grids of the islands whose domains it
touches, plus the river segments bucketed near it, in tile-relative coordinates. A
segment only shapes ground within its floor width plus a fixed reach. Its banks rise
far above any terrain before that reach, so neighbouring tiles agree exactly.

**Water** (`shaders/water.gdshader`, one shader for ocean, lakes and rivers).
- **Geometry:** six Gerstner waves, each faded where it is too short for the local mesh
  spacing.
- **Normals:** analytic, plus 24 small analytic waves from a deep-water spectrum spread
  around the wind. Each is faded by the pixel footprint, so distant water averages out
  instead of sparkling.
- **Lighting:** Godot's lighting gives the sky reflection and sun glints. The shader adds
  the light coming up through the water: the refracted background absorbed with depth
  (red first), light scattered in the water body and through thin crests, and a Schlick
  Fresnel. Rivers and lakes are siltier.
- **Foam:** breaks on pinched crests, along the swash line and over fast shallow riffles.
- **Rivers:** they carry their flow in UV. Their waves run downstream, and the whole
  pattern is advected along the current with two copies half a cycle apart, cross-faded.
  So rivers visibly flow the right way everywhere.

## Layout

- `scripts/main.gd` builds the scene: sky, fog, sun, ocean, world, player, HUD.
- `scripts/archipelago.gd` places islands; `island.gd`, `island_sim.gd` and `meander.gd`
  simulate each island and build its lakes and rivers.
- `scripts/terrain_world.gd` streams 64 m near chunks (mesh, height-map collision, river
  and lake water, digging) and 1 km far tiles out to the horizon.
- `scripts/terrain_generator.gd` / `terrain_mesher.gd` gather each tile's primitives, run
  the GPU pipeline on a worker thread and build the meshes.
- `scripts/player.gd`, `scripts/hud.gd` handle the controller, interaction and hotbar.
- `tools/heightmap_preview.gd` renders hillshaded previews of the final terrain:
  `godot --path . -s tools/heightmap_preview.gd -- <out_dir> <spacing> <tiles> <x> <z>`.
- `tools/island_preview.gd` renders an island's simulated grid with its rivers, oxbows
  and lakes: `godot --path . -s tools/island_preview.gd -- <out_dir> <x> <z> <px> <pick>`.
- `tools/meander_test.gd` runs the meander model on a straight valley:
  `godot --path . -s tools/meander_test.gd -- <out.png> <width> <length> <belt>`.
- `tools/shot.tscn` is a scripted playthrough that saves screenshots.
